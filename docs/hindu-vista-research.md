## Brand identity

The brand PDF is `Downloads\rvyWXxsqwnEC1mezSHvyiRxyAG_hXWRmOHvoqPdDabY.pdf`, dated 24 November 2026. It is a three-page typography sheet.

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep Slate | `#2E4053` | Navigation, important bands, footer, Latin letters |
| Sun Gold | `#F1C40F` | The Hindi ह in the wordmark, and small highlights |
| Warm White | `#FCFAF6` | Page background and inverse lettering |
| Latin wordmark | Playfair Display SemiBold | VI and TA |
| Hindi glyph | Arya Bold | The central ह, and the name हिन्दुविस्टा |

The lockup reads VI + gold ह + TA, with a circular ह seal. The name line is “Hinduvista हिन्दुविस्टा”. On Deep Slate the Latin letters are warm white and the ह stays gold. Sage (`#70858A`) and terracotta (`#C3654C`) are marked as alternate colourways only. The core palette remains Deep Slate and Sun Gold.

The public mark follows that lockup in type. It is not a traced redraw of the PDF artwork.

Gold is not used for small body text on warm white. Small labels on ivory use a darker gold (`#8A6D12`) so they remain readable. Bright gold sits on Deep Slate, or as a button fill with dark text.

## Destination research

Sample cities are Jaipur, Udaipur, Agra, Delhi, Varanasi, and Mysuru. They are labelled on the site as demonstration content and are not described as Hindu Vista departures.

Authoritative pages read while preparing the copy:

- Taj Mahal, UNESCO World Heritage List 252: white-marble mausoleum on the bank of the Yamuna, ordered by Shah Jahan for Mumtaz Mahal; mausoleum construction given as 1631–1648, with mosque, guest house and gateway completed by 1653; Ustad Ahmad Lahori named as the main architect. <https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/252/>
- Agra Fort, UNESCO World Heritage List 251: red-sandstone Mughal fort near the Taj gardens, enclosure walls of about 2.5 km, with palaces and audience halls including work for Shah Jahan. <https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/251/>
- Jantar Mantar, Jaipur, UNESCO World Heritage List 1338, inscribed 2010: early 18th-century observatory built for Jai Singh II, with about twenty main fixed instruments for naked-eye observation; described as the best preserved of India’s historic observatories. <https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1338/>

Other notes use long-published identifications and are written so they can be edited:

- Jaipur was founded in 1727 by Sawai Jai Singh II. Amber Fort is part of the Hill Forts of Rajasthan (UNESCO list 247, 2013). Hawa Mahal was built in 1799 for Sawai Pratap Singh.
- Humayun’s Tomb (UNESCO 232) was completed in 1570 for Bega Begum. The Qutb complex (UNESCO 233) was begun under Qutb-ud-Din Aibak. The Red Fort, Delhi (UNESCO 231) is Shah Jahan’s citadel in Shahjahanabad. India Gate, designed by Edwin Lutyens, was inaugurated in 1931 as the All India War Memorial.
- Udaipur was founded in 1559 by Maharana Udai Singh II. The City Palace stands on Lake Pichola. Jag Mandir is associated with Prince Khurram’s refuge at the Mewar court.
- Kashi Vishwanath is a Jyotirlinga; the present temple is associated with Ahilyabai Holkar’s rebuilding in 1780. Sarnath is the traditional site of the Buddha’s first sermon; the lion capital from the site is India’s national emblem and is kept in the site museum. Dashashwamedh Ghat holds an evening Ganga aarti. No clock time is printed.
- Mysore Palace (Amba Vilas) is the early twentieth-century Wodeyar palace designed by Henry Irwin after fire destroyed the previous building. Chamundeshwari on Chamundi Hill is the tutelary goddess of that house. St. Philomena’s Cathedral is a twentieth-century neo-Gothic church.

The Incredible India attractions index was the requested reference: <https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/attractions>. Monument wording used for the demo follows the UNESCO texts above where a page was fetched, and otherwise stays with identifications that can be corrected in the dashboard.

Seasonal lines such as “October to March” are general climate guidance for visiting these regions. They are not a Hindu Vista operating calendar.

Sightseeing sequences are editorial. They do not state distances, driving times, or a claim that the order is a geographical route. Visiting durations are blank unless a verified duration is entered later.

No ticket prices, opening hours, or ritual times are copied onto the pages, because those change.

## Image sourcing

Landmark pictures are verified Wikimedia Commons photographs saved as local WebP files. They are not AI-generated stand-ins for famous buildings. Generation was not used, because an invented view of the Taj Mahal, Hawa Mahal, or a palace would misrepresent the building.

Each file’s creator, licence, and Commons page is listed in [image-manifest.md](image-manifest.md) and on the public credits page. Commons licences in this set are Creative Commons or public domain. Attribution is the credits page.

Admin uploads are stored under `public/uploads` and recorded in the database. They are separate from the Commons set.

No third-party hotlink is used as a permanent image address.
